Scope: Flow

Use Flow when the decisions to change span the connected views of one named task: booking, checkout, signup, onboarding, editing, or recovery. A flow is judged by whether a person can get from its entry points to the result they came for, so record its entry points, its commitment points, and where it returns before anything else. Each decision point has its own question, and the information needed for each commitment must be available before that commitment, not after it. State matters more here than anywhere else: what persists between steps, what happens on going back, interruption, error recovery, and return, and wording that distinguishes unsaved, pending, and committed states across the sequence. Build each view's components from real products that handle the same decision, and keep type, color, and layout relationships consistent from step to step so the sequence stays recognizable. Product-wide foundations are retained identity unless the assignment opens them. Other journeys and nearby shared areas stay outside scope unless the task needs them. Verify the whole task from each entry point through every commitment, including going back, interruption, error recovery, and return, at desktop, tablet, and phone widths.

Parent: Product
Included: The connected decisions, views, controls, states, feedback, interruption, recovery, and return needed for the named task.
Boundary: Other journeys and nearby shared areas remain outside scope unless needed by the assigned task.
Record in step 0: The task's entry points, its commitment points, and where it returns.

At this scope
Question: The question the whole task answers, and the question at each decision point along it.
Content and order: Every decision, view, action, state, and decision prerequisite in the task, including interruption, recovery, return, and what persists between steps; the order within each view and across the sequence, so that what is needed for each commitment is available before it.
Components: The job at each decision and view, the components and controls ported from real products for those jobs, the commitment points, and wording that distinguishes unsaved, pending, and committed states across the sequence.
Foundations: The pillars the flow opens. Product-wide foundations are retained identity unless the assignment opens them. Text roles and color uses and state roles stay consistent from step to step.
Layout: Each view in the flow, with shared relationships that keep the sequence recognizable.
Behavior: Feedback, validation, interruption, recovery, return, persistence, responsive changes, and transitions between steps.
Verify: The whole task from each entry point through every commitment, including going back, interruption, error recovery, and return, at desktop, tablet, and phone widths.

Expansion: If the task cannot work without changing another journey or a shared area, identify the specific conflict and the smallest required expansion.
