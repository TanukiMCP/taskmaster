Scope and situation matrix

This file selects how a design request uses the procedure in process.txt. The procedure is the same for every assignment: it holds the methods, rules, and order of intentional design. What changes is the working path. The situation decides that path—what kind of job this is, which questions it must answer, and in what order—and its SOP lives in situations/. The scope decides only how far the work reaches, and its sheet lives in scopes/. Every request maps to one or more entries, each one situation and one scope. Each entry is a ready composition of operations that the agent adapts to the assignment before any work is done, and Taskmaster runs it.

Prompt selector

Choose the situation and the smallest scope containing the decisions the task needs to change. The brief supplies the goal, required behavior, and explicit constraints. Device, platform, audience, existing identity, available assets, and stage of completion are task conditions. Record them with the selection.

Select the situation by answering these questions in order and stopping at the first that applies:

1. Does the work to be designed exist yet? If neither it nor a product around it exists, the situation is Create.
2. Is only inspection, diagnosis, or recommendation requested? The situation is Review.
3. Is a capability, content, view, state, or variant being added to an existing product? The situation is Extend. A missing variant—another screen size, theme, language, or content volume—is an addition.
4. Should what exists be replaced—its structure, sections, components, or treatment reconsidered from the question up? The situation is Redesign.
5. Should what exists stay and be repaired—a specific observed problem or a named weakness, with the rest of the work sound? The situation is Fix.

Redesign replaces what is there, using it as evidence. Fix keeps what is there and repairs it. When a repair turns out to need the structure replaced, the Fix escalates to a Redesign rather than quietly widening.

Select the scope as the smallest unit whose boundary contains every decision the task needs to change. Scope follows the effect of a change, not the number or location of files edited. The scopes form a ladder from the smallest unit on a view to the whole product, plus one scope that cuts across it:

| Rung | Scope | Covers | Parent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Element | One piece of text, one control, one icon, or one image | Component or Section |
| 2 | Component | One self-contained piece made of elements: a card, table, form, tab panel, modal | Section or Page or view |
| 3 | Section | One band or region of a page that does a single job there: a hero, an ingredients list, a pricing table | Page or view |
| 4 | Page or view | One screen and the sections on it | Flow or Product |
| 5 | Flow | The connected views of one named task: booking, checkout, signup | Product |
| 6 | Product | The whole application or site | None |
| — | Shared styles and assets | One foundation applied across every rung: typography, palette, illustration family, icons, surfaces, motion | Product |

Whether a unit should exist at all is decided by its parent. Every entry asks that question first of the unit it targets; when the answer is that the unit should be removed or moved, the entry escalates to the parent rung instead of improving it.

| Situation / Scope | Element | Component | Section | Page or view | Flow | Product | Shared styles and assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Create | A new control or text role for a new design | A new reusable component | A new section for a new page | A standalone page or view | A new task sequence | A new application or site | A new type system, palette, icon set, or identity |
| Extend | A new state or variant of a button | A new variant or state of a component | A new section on an existing page | A new page in an existing product, or a variant of one | A new step or branch in an existing journey | A new feature area in an existing product | Dark mode, a new illustration subject, new icons in an existing family |
| Redesign | "Rethink this call to action" | "This container looks poorly designed" | "Redesign the ingredients section" | "Redesign the landing page" | "Rebuild checkout" | "Overhaul the whole app" | "Our typography feels generic" |
| Fix | "This button label is unclear" | "This modal overflows on phones" | "The reviews band breaks at tablet width" | "The pricing page breaks at tablet width" | "Users lose their entries when they go back" | "Navigation is inconsistent across sections" | "Text on the accent color fails contrast" |
| Review | "Is this label right?" | "What's wrong with this card?" | "Should this section be here?" | "Audit the landing page" | "Walk through signup and report problems" | "Review the whole product's design" | "Assess our palette's accessibility" |

Every entry is available. The examples show typical requests; the selection questions decide.

What each situation permits

| Situation | The agent may change | It must preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Create | Establish the unresolved structure, controls, interactions, composition, typography, palette, imagery, and other decisions needed for the assigned scope. | The brief, supplied content and identity, technical constraints, and required relationships with surrounding work. Freedom applies to decisions that are open. |
| Extend | Add the requested capability or content, its necessary views and states, and missing variants. Introduce a new treatment where an existing one cannot serve the new requirement. | Suitable existing visual relationships, interaction conventions, terminology, and behavior. The addition does not authorize an unrelated restyling. |
| Redesign | Replace the structure, sections, components, controls, navigation, hierarchy, composition, copy, and visual treatment within scope. The existing work's content and jobs are evidence; its form is not a constraint. | Required capabilities, factual meaning, user data, explicit constraints, and useful connections to the surrounding product, and the retained identity unless the assignment opens it. A redesign does not authorize inventing services or dropping requirements. |
| Fix | Diagnose and correct the specified problem's cause and directly affected dependencies, including layout, controls, state transitions, or shared implementation where needed. | The existing structure and design, unrelated design decisions, and working behavior. The cause may be unknown; the target problem is specific. |
| Review | Inspect, operate, compare, diagnose, capture evidence, use temporary test input, and recommend changes. | The product and real user data. Findings do not authorize implementation or changes to the procedure. |

Create establishes missing design; Extend carries established design into an addition. Redesign replaces what exists from the question up; Fix repairs what exists and keeps the rest. Review authorizes examination and recommendations.

How each situation works

Each situation's SOP gives its working path in full. In short:

| Situation | Working path |
|---|---|
| Create | From the brief, through every procedure step in order, deciding each one; supplied content and identity are kept where they belong. |
| Extend | Inspect and record what the existing work it joins already decides, then decide only what is new and keep the foundations. |
| Redesign | Inspect the existing work as evidence and capture it, then rebuild through every step, asking whether each existing section should exist; nothing survives only because it was built. |
| Fix | Reproduce and capture the problem, trace it to the responsible step, correct the cause there, and carry the correction only through the steps it directly affects. |
| Review | Establish what the question requires, record what the existing work holds, walk the task, and deliver findings; nothing is changed. |

How scope limits the work

| Scope | Included | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Element | The element's content, wording in every state, kind of control or text role, treatment, size, spacing, target size, focus, keyboard operation, and feedback. | The component or section around it, its siblings, and the product's foundations are outside scope; they are inputs the element must fit. |
| Component | Its anatomy, content presentation, meaningful states, interactions, accessibility, responsive behavior, and intended uses when it is reusable. | Surrounding page composition and unrelated components remain outside scope. An assignment targeting one occurrence does not automatically include other occurrences. |
| Section | The section's job on its page, the components inside it and their jobs, its internal order and grouping, its heading and wording, its imagery, and its responsive treatment. | Other sections, the page's overall order, and the product's foundations are outside scope. Whether the section should exist at all is decided by its page. |
| Page or view | Its composition, content organization, sections and their existence, local controls, imagery, responsive treatment, states, entry, and onward connections. | Neighboring destinations, the entire journey, and global restyling are not automatically included. |
| Flow | The connected decisions, views, controls, states, feedback, interruption, recovery, and return needed for the named task. | Other journeys and nearby shared areas remain outside scope unless needed by the assigned task. |
| Product | Product-wide structure, connected journeys, shared design foundations, and relationships among the parts. | Scope does not authorize a new product purpose, invented requirements, removal of required capabilities, or unrelated infrastructure replacement. |
| Shared styles and assets | The named foundation, its relationships, and its application across intended uses. This may be typography, palette, illustration family, iconography, surfaces, motion, or the shared visual identity. | Other foundations and independent page redesigns stay outside scope. Directly affected wrapping, spacing, framing, or presentation can change as necessary to apply the assigned foundation. |

Boundaries shared by every entry

Explicit task instructions govern what can change. The situation determines which decisions can be reopened; scope determines their reach.

Necessary supporting changes are allowed when they make the requested work function, preserve required behavior, or keep directly affected uses coherent. An unrelated improvement does not become authorized because the agent prefers it. Scope follows the effect of a change, not the number or location of files edited.

A local change should be contained where possible rather than silently changing the whole product. A reusable-component fix includes checking affected uses. A typography change includes directly affected wrapping and spacing. A review stops before implementation.

If the goal cannot be achieved within its boundary, identify the specific conflict and the smallest required expansion. This is for a real scope conflict, not routine approval pauses. In Taskmaster, the expansion is an escalation: the agent composes again with the reason, and the operations of the entry for the parent scope or the right situation replace the current entry's remaining operations.

Scope changes the amount of the product being considered, not the standard of care. Effort follows consequential unresolved decisions, not equal time per page or step. Preserve the procedure's intermediate results and step announcements; add no routine reports, scorecards, or sets of alternative designs.

Requests that span several entries

A request that needs more than one entry becomes one entry per situation and scope, run in sequence, each with its own operations. "Redesign the landing page and add a pricing page" is Redesign × Page or view, then Extend × Page or view. Order the entries so that each one's starting material exists: shared foundations before the views that use them, and a redesign before an addition that should inherit it.

A Review ends with findings. Acting on them requires the user's authorization and adds a separate Fix or Redesign entry whose first operation needs the findings.

How Taskmaster runs an entry

1. Record the request, the end goal as the result to produce, the value that makes it worth having, and the task conditions.
2. Read each entry's ready composition with library('design/<situation>/<scope>'): the situation SOP, the scope sheet, and one operation per SOP step with the operations it needs and what it produces.
3. Before any work, adopt it with compose: for each operation, write the specific work it will do on this assignment and the questions it must answer, and make what it produces specific. For several entries, prefix each entry's operation ids with its name and have the first operation of a later entry also need the last operation of the one before.
4. Taskmaster issues the operations one at a time, each with the work written for it, the earlier results it builds on, the procedure steps it works in from process.txt in full, and the scope's reach for those steps. Record each operation's actual result before the next.
5. When a verify operation finds a discrepancy, revise the operation that works in the responsible procedure step; the operations built on it are redone or confirmed. When nothing remains to correct, record the verify operation as done.
6. When a step shows the decision belongs to a parent scope or another situation, escalate: compose again with the reason, putting the right entry's operations in place of the current entry's remaining ones and carrying the goal.
