Situation: Redesign

Use Redesign when what exists should be replaced, not repaired: the structure, sections, components, or treatment of the work in scope are being reconsidered from the question up. Redesign treats the existing work as evidence, not as a starting structure. It tells you what content, data, and capabilities exist, which jobs people rely on, and where the current design fails the question, but its sections, layout, and components carry no weight just because they were built. Begin by stating the question and recording what must survive: required capabilities, factual meaning, user data, explicit constraints, and useful connections to the surrounding product. Whether the existing identity is open is a task condition; record it, and when identity is not open, keep it through the visual steps and research only what it does not already resolve. Then inspect the existing work as it stands at desktop, tablet, and phone widths—what attracts attention first, what appears grouped, what can be read, and where it fails—and capture it before you change anything. Rebuild from content and actions, then apply the existence question to every existing section so that nothing survives only because it was there. Name the job each section must do, look at how real products do each job, and adapt the component they use; an existing component stays only when it already does the job best, stated as a kept decision with its reason. Every later step is reconsidered because its inputs changed, not only where it looked weak. Verify against the question and against the before captures, confirming that nothing required was lost; a newly found earlier cause becomes the place to rerun from. Redesign does not authorize inventing services or dropping requirements.

May change: Replace the structure, sections, components, controls, navigation, hierarchy, composition, copy, and visual treatment within scope. The existing work's content and jobs are evidence; its form is not a constraint.
Must preserve: Required capabilities, factual meaning, user data, explicit constraints, and useful connections to the surrounding product, and the retained identity unless the assignment opens it. A redesign does not authorize inventing services or dropping requirements.

SOP

1. State the question and what must survive [process 0]
Id: question
Produces: The question, the end goal, what must survive the redesign, and whether the existing identity is open.
State the question and the end goal. Record what must survive the redesign—required capabilities, factual meaning, user data, explicit constraints, and connections to the surrounding product—and whether the existing identity is open.

2. Inspect the existing work as evidence [process 10] (diagnose)
Id: evidence
Needs: question
Produces: Before captures at desktop, tablet, and phone widths, and a record of the existing content, data, capabilities, jobs, and where the work fails the question.
Run the verification walk on the existing work as it stands at desktop, tablet, and phone widths: what attracts attention first, what appears grouped, what can be read, and where it fails the question. Record the content, data, capabilities, and jobs it serves, and save before captures inside the work folder. This is evidence for the rebuild, not a structure to keep.

3. Re-establish the content and actions [process 1]
Id: content
Needs: question, evidence
Produces: The objects, actions, states, and decision prerequisites, with the required content and capabilities carried over.
Establish the objects, actions, states, and decision prerequisites from the question, using the existing content as evidence. Carry over the required content and capabilities; nothing else is carried by default.

4. Decide what should exist and rank it [process 2]
Id: order
Needs: question, content
Produces: Each existing section kept, removed, or relocated with its reason, and a ranked order of what remains for each task.
Apply the existence question to every existing section and region before ranking anything. Record what is removed or relocated and why, then rank what remains for each task.

5. Name the jobs and adapt components [process 3]
Id: components
Needs: content, order
Produces: The job inventory: each section and its jobs, the real product or design system inspected for each job, and the component adapted from it, or kept with its reason.
Name the job each section must do, look at how real products do each job, and adapt the component they use. Keep an existing component only when it already does the job best, stated as a kept decision with its reason.

6. Research the opened pillars [process 4]
Id: research
Needs: question, components
Produces: A linked, inspected source for each opened pillar, and the retained identity named for the pillars it still resolves.
Research the pillars the redesign opens from human-made sources. When identity is not open, keep it in the pillars it resolves and research only what it does not.

7. Set the typography [process 5]
Id: typography
Needs: order, components, research
Produces: Font files and text roles rendered with the real content, their line lengths, and the usable widths layout will take.
Set type for the new structure, keeping retained families and scale when identity is not open, and render the real content to give layout its widths.

8. Produce the imagery [process 6]
Id: imagery
Needs: research, typography
Produces: Imagery produced or kept for the new structure, resolved at its actual sizes beside the actual text.
Produce or keep imagery for the new structure. A kept asset pack still has to serve the new sections at their actual sizes.

9. Construct the color [process 7]
Id: color
Needs: order, research, imagery
Produces: Color roles and readable pairs for the new structure, rendered in place with the real type and imagery.
Assign color roles and readable pairs for the new structure, keeping the retained palette when identity is not open.

10. Lay out the views [process 8]
Id: layout
Needs: order, components, typography, imagery, color
Produces: The working arrangement at each width: widths, spans, spacing, framing, icons, and surfaces, built from the adapted components.
Construct the arrangement from the rendered content dimensions, building each section from the components adapted in SOP step 5.

11. Complete the behavior [process 9]
Id: behavior
Needs: content, components, layout
Produces: Every action, state, responsive change, and motion the new structure needs, working, with the required capabilities preserved.
Implement every action, state, responsive change, and purposeful motion the new structure needs, preserving the required capabilities.

12. Verify against the question and the evidence [process 10] (verify)
Id: verify
Needs: question, evidence, behavior
Produces: What was checked against the question and the before captures, with every required capability, piece of content, and connection confirmed present.
Follow the task through entry, decision, action, feedback, and return at desktop, tablet, and phone widths. Compare with the before captures and the list of what must survive: every required capability, piece of content, and connection is still present. Record it as done, or revise the operation that works in the procedure step responsible for the earliest discrepancy.

Completion: Deliver the redesigned work, how to reach it, before and after captures saved inside the work folder, what was replaced, removed, or kept and why, what was checked, and any remaining limitations.
